Setup 3 — 2D radial swarm with central trap

A disk ("petri dish") of \(N\) cells initialized uniformly on the annulus \(\tilde R_{\text{trap}} < \tilde r < \tilde R_{\text{dish}}\). One or more Gaussian chemoattractant pulses are launched from the center at times \(\tilde t_j = j\,\Delta\tilde t_{\text{wave}}\) (\(j = 0, \dots, n_{\text{waves}} - 1\)) and propagate radially outward at nondim speed \(C\). This is a 2D extension of Setup 2: polarization is now a vector \(\mathbf{P}_i = (P_{xi}, P_{yi})\) and the cue is radially symmetric.

The cue field is a sum of radially-symmetric Gaussian pulses, each launched at time \(\tilde t_j\) and propagating outward at speed \(C\):

\[ \mathcal{L}(\tilde r, \tilde t) = \sum_{j} \mathcal{L}_j(\tilde r, \tilde t), \qquad \mathcal{L}_j(\tilde r, \tilde t) = M\,e^{-(\tilde r - C(\tilde t - \tilde t_j))^2/2} \;\Theta(\tilde t - \tilde t_j). \]

Since \(\mathcal{L}\) depends on position only through \(\tilde r = |\tilde{\mathbf{X}}|\), its 2D gradient is purely radial: \(\nabla\mathcal{L} = (\partial_{\tilde r}\mathcal{L})\,\hat r\), where

\[ \partial_{\tilde r}\mathcal{L} = \sum_{j} \partial_{\tilde r}\mathcal{L}_j = -\sum_{j} \mathcal{L}_j \cdot \bigl(\tilde r - C(\tilde t - \tilde t_j)\bigr). \]

The polarization SDE and position ODE for each free cell \(i\) are

\[ d\mathbf{P}_i = \Bigl[\tilde\chi\,\nabla\mathcal{L} + (\mathcal{L}-1)\,\mathbf{P}_i + \lambda\,(|\mathbf{P}_i|^2 - |\mathbf{P}_i|^4)\,\mathbf{P}_i \Bigr]d\tilde t + \sqrt{2\,\vartheta\,\mathcal{L}\,d\tilde t}\;\boldsymbol{\eta}_i, \qquad \frac{d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i}{d\tilde t} = \tilde\mu\,\mathbf{P}_i, \]

where \(\boldsymbol{\eta}_i\) is a 2D iid Gaussian per step (Euler–Maruyama). Trapped cells (\(\tilde r_i < \tilde R_{\text{trap}}\)) have frozen position but continuing polarization dynamics. Nondim groups (identical to Setup 2):

\(M = L_{\max}/L_c\)peak cue / threshold \(C = c/(\sigma\,r_0\,L_c)\)wave speed (\(\sigma\) per \(t_0\)) \(\tilde\chi\)chemotactic coupling \(\tilde\mu\)cell motility \(\lambda\)well depth (intrinsic) \(\vartheta\)noise strength (intrinsic)

Anti-wave regime (biological interest): at moderate \(\tilde\chi\) and appropriate \(\lambda, \vartheta\), GL cells drift against the wave propagation direction — here inward toward the trap — mimicking the chemotactic recruitment of neutrophils toward a central Candida source.

Hill / linear-relaxation alternative model

Selecting the Hill model in the controls replaces the GL polarization SDE with a 2D linear-relaxation form, and the linear velocity \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}} = \tilde\mu\,\mathbf{P}\) with a Hill-saturating response:

\[ d\mathbf{P}_i = \bigl[\tilde\chi\,\nabla\mathcal{L} - \mathbf{P}_i\bigr]d\tilde t + \sqrt{2\,\vartheta\,\mathcal{L}\,d\tilde t}\,\boldsymbol{\eta}_i, \qquad \frac{d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i}{d\tilde t} = \tilde v_{0}\,\frac{|\mathbf{P}_i|^{n}}{1 + |\mathbf{P}_i|^{n}}\,\hat{\mathbf{P}}_i. \]

\(\tilde\chi\) and \(\vartheta\) are shared between models. \(\tilde v_{0}\) and the Hill exponent \(n \in \{1, \ldots, 10\}\) replace \(\tilde\mu\); \(\lambda\) has no Hill analog. The trap, dish, and multi-wave cue are unchanged. See ../docs/physics/hill_model.md for the nondimensionalization.
