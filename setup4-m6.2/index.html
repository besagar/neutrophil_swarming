Setup 4d — Quorum-throttled relay · M6.2

Same dish, same cells and same LTB4 relay as Setup 4a, and the same basal density signal as Setup 4c — but the signal acts at a different point in the loop. In M6.1 adenosine raises the relay threshold; here the quorum signal \(\mathcal{Q}\) leaves the threshold at \(L_0\) and instead throttles the production rate of LTB4. Both are quorum sensing; they are not the same model, and the Hill exponent \(m\) is what separates them.

Model M6.2 (quorum-throttled production). Dimensionally,

\[ \partial_t L = D_L \Delta L + \frac{a_0}{1+(Q/Q_0)^m}\,\frac{\sigma}{h}\,\Theta(L-L_0), \qquad \partial_t Q = D_Q \Delta Q + \frac{b\,\sigma}{h} - \gamma_Q Q, \]

or, at the agent level, with each cell a discrete source,

\[ \partial_t L = D_L \Delta L + \frac{a_0}{h}\sum_i \delta_{2D}(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_i)\, \frac{\Theta\bigl(L(\mathbf r_i)-L_0\bigr)}{1+\bigl(Q(\mathbf r_i)/Q_0\bigr)^m}, \qquad \partial_t Q = D_Q \Delta Q + \frac{b}{h}\sum_i \delta_{2D}(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_i) - \gamma_Q Q. \]

Nondimensionalisation. Same intrinsic scales as M1 and M6.1, with \(\mathcal{Q}\) measured in the physical half-saturation constant \(Q_0\):

\[ \ell_0 = \frac{a_0}{L_0 D_L},\quad t_0 = \frac{\ell_0^2}{D_L} = \frac{a_0^2}{L_0^2 D_L^3},\quad \mathcal{L} = \frac{L}{L_0},\quad \mathcal{Q} = \frac{Q}{Q_0}, \] \[ \alpha = \frac{a_0}{h D_L L_0} = \frac1{\tilde h},\quad \beta = \frac{b}{h D_L Q_0},\quad D = \frac{D_Q}{D_L},\quad \gamma = \frac{a_0^2\,\gamma_Q}{L_0^2 D_L^3} = \gamma_Q t_0, \] \[ \partial_{\tilde t}\mathcal{L} = \tilde\nabla^2\mathcal{L} + \alpha\sum_i \tilde\delta^2(\tilde{\mathbf r}-\tilde{\mathbf r}_i)\, H^+(\mathcal{L}_i;1;n_L)\,H^-(\mathcal{Q}_i;1;m), \qquad \partial_{\tilde t}\mathcal{Q} = D\,\tilde\nabla^2\mathcal{Q} + \beta\sum_i \tilde\delta^2(\tilde{\mathbf r}-\tilde{\mathbf r}_i) - \gamma\,\mathcal{Q}. \]

The step \(\Theta(L-L_0)\) is Hill-regularised as \(H^+(\mathcal{L};1;n_L)\) (repo-wide convention), and the throttle is exactly a negative Hill factor, \(1/(1+\mathcal{Q}^m) = H^-(\mathcal{Q};1;m)\), with its own independent exponent \(m\). Setting \(\beta = 0\) recovers M1 exactly — note that \(m = 0\) does not (it gives a uniform factor \(\tfrac12\)); \(\beta\) is the single off-dial.

What the mechanism does. The quorum source is unconditional, so \(\mathcal{Q}\) relaxes to a spatially uniform tone — behind the front, at it, and ahead of it:

\[ \mathcal{Q}_{\text{ss}} = \frac{\beta\,\tilde\sigma}{\gamma} \qquad\text{(2D–2D)}. \]

Because that tone is uniform, M6.2 is exactly M1 with a rescaled source — the threshold stays at \(\mathcal{L}_c = 1\) and the per-cell emission rate becomes

\[ \alpha_{\text{eff}}(\tilde\sigma) = \frac{\alpha}{1+\mathcal{Q}_{\text{ss}}^{\,m}} = \frac{\alpha}{1+(\beta\tilde\sigma/\gamma)^m}. \]

This is exact (given the uniform tone), and it is as far as the analysis goes here. The drive per unit area, \(\alpha_{\text{eff}}\tilde\sigma\), grows like \(\tilde\sigma\) at low density, saturates when \(m = 1\), and falls like \(\tilde\sigma^{1-m}\) once \(\mathcal{Q}_{\text{ss}}\gg1\) for \(m > 1\). How the relay speed follows from that drive is not something we quote a formula for — the mean-field pushed-front flux balance does not describe this system reliably. Run the density sweep and read the speed off the measured points.

Relation to M6.1 — and how to tell them apart. Both models feed the same basal, density-proportional tone into the relay, one at the threshold and one at the production rate, and at \(m = 1\) they enter their respective balances the same way (with \(\lambda_A \leftrightarrow \beta\), \(\tilde\Gamma_A \leftrightarrow \gamma\)) — so sweeping both at \(m = 1\) is a useful cross-check that they behave alike. They separate through:

  1. \(m \neq 1\). Only M6.2 can make the drive \(\alpha_{\text{eff}}\tilde\sigma\) fall with density (\(m > 1\)). If that carries through to the front speed, a local density excess propagates slower than its surroundings and the Höfer–Maini streaming feedback changes sign rather than merely weakening — something M6.1's threshold shift cannot do. Whether the measured \(\tilde c(\tilde\sigma)\) really turns over is exactly what the sweep is for.
  2. Failure mode at high density. M6.1 fails by threshold — cells never reach \(1+\lambda_A\mathcal{A}\) and nothing ignites. M6.2 fails by starvation — cells do cross threshold and do fire, just too weakly to drag the next shell over, so the front slows to a crawl. On the dish: M6.1 goes dark, M6.2 shows many dim emitters and a stalled front.
  3. Wake amplitude. \(\mathcal{L}\) behind the front scales with \(\alpha_{\text{eff}}\) in M6.2 (visibly lower at high \(\tilde\sigma\)) but is unchanged in M6.1.

Geometry (2D–2D vs 2D–3D). In 2D–3D both fields diffuse in the half-space above the cell floor (thin-slab grid: explicit in-plane diffusion + Crank–Nicolson in \(z\)); cells sit at \(z=0\) and read \(\mathcal{Q}|_{z=0}\). Two things change: the bare relay's own density scaling, and the tone, which becomes the screened surface value. Because \(Q_0\) is a physical constant it cannot absorb \(b/h\) the way M6.1's arbitrary unit \(A_0\) does, so the surface-source coefficient carries an explicit \(\tilde h\):

\[ \beta_{3D} = \beta\,\tilde h = \frac{b}{D_L Q_0 \ell_0}, \qquad \mathcal{Q}_{\text{ss}}(z=0) = \frac{\beta_{3D}\,\tilde\sigma}{\sqrt{D\gamma}}. \]

The \(\beta\) slider is always the 2D–2D group above; the \(\tilde h\) factor is applied internally when Geometry is 2D–3D.

Anti-streaming. As in M6.1, weakening the density dependence of the speed only suppresses channels if \(\mathcal{Q}\) resolves local density fluctuations: the screening length \(\tilde\ell_Q = \sqrt{D/\gamma}\) should be of order the relay length (\(\approx 1 = \ell_0\)). If \(\tilde\ell_Q\) is much larger, the throttle acts as a near-global rescaling of \(\alpha\) and a local bump still outruns its surroundings.

Limitation — no self-termination. Basal-only production gives no arrest: \(H^-\) is positive for every finite \(\mathcal{Q}\), so cells behind the front keep firing (weakly) and the wave is a propagating / filling front, not a finite ring. For a finite radius as well, combine with a wake-accumulating (M4-style, LTB4-triggered) inhibitor; the two arms act ahead of vs. behind the front and do not interfere.

GL cell SDE (Euler–Maruyama). Polarization \(\mathbf{P}_i\) and position \(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i\) are unchanged from M1 — the throttle acts only on relay emission:

\[ d\mathbf{P}_i = \Bigl[ \kappa\,\tilde{\nabla}\mathcal{L} + \lambda\,(\mathcal{L} - \mathcal{L}_c)\,\mathbf{P}_i + \nu\,(|\mathbf{P}_i|^2 - |\mathbf{P}_i|^4)\,\mathbf{P}_i \Bigr]d\tilde{t} + \sqrt{2\,\vartheta\,\mathcal{L}\,d\tilde{t}}\;\boldsymbol{\eta}_i, \qquad \frac{d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i}{d\tilde{t}} = \mu\,\mathbf{P}_i. \]

Initial conditions. As in M1, a time-limited firing source inside \(\tilde r < \tilde r_{\text{fire}}\) for \(\tilde t < \tilde t_{\text{fire}}\) nucleates the wave; it is an external stimulus and is deliberately not throttled by \(\mathcal{Q}\). The quorum field itself starts at its steady tone \(\mathcal{Q}_{\text{ss}}\) (toggle in the controls): starting from zero, it would take \(\tilde t\sim1/\gamma\) to fill, and at small \(\gamma\) the run would silently measure unthrottled (M1) physics.

Try this. Sweep at \(\beta=0\) for the bare-relay reference, then at \(m=1\) with \(\beta\) matched to the M6.1 page's \(\lambda_A\) (and \(\gamma=\tilde\Gamma_A\)) — the two pages should behave alike. Then set \(m=2\) and re-sweep: watch whether the measured points turn over. Meanwhile watch the dish as you raise \(\beta\): cells keep flashing, but dimmer — emission opacity is the throttle factor \(H^-(\mathcal{Q};1;m)\).

Target — “stick to the target”. A circle of radius \(\tilde{R}_{\text{target}}\) at the dish centre stands for the object the swarm converges on (a candida cluster, a bead, a sterile injury). It is a one-way, absorbing inner boundary: the first time a cell reaches \(\tilde r_i \le \tilde{R}_{\text{target}}\) it is projected onto the circle and frozen there for the rest of the run — it has engaged the target. A stuck cell still polarizes and still emits the cue, so the cue model above is untouched; only \(d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i/d\tilde{t} = \mu\mathbf{P}_i\) is suppressed. The target disk is excluded from the initial cell placement, so no cell starts engaged. Switch it off in the controls to recover the plain dish (reflective outer wall only). Engaged cells are ringed in teal on the dish, and the on target KPI is the recruitment curve. See setup4_swarm3d.md §2c.

Interaction paradigm. Set all parameters, press Calculate, then scrub the time slider once the run finishes.

Numerical methods

\(\mathcal{L}\) and \(\mathcal{Q}\) share one solver (M1's relay stepper plus a generic auxiliary reaction–diffusion field, also used by M6.1): explicit Euler for in-plane diffusion at CFL \(d\tilde t_{\text{sub}}\le\Delta\tilde x^2/(4D)\), with the quorum source smeared by the same bilinear PIC weights as the relay source. Each cell's throttle is evaluated against the pre-step \(\mathcal{Q}\). In 2D–3D both fields are advanced on the 3D thin-slab grid (Crank–Nicolson in \(z\) on the non-uniform exponential grid), \(\mathcal{L}\) with \((D{=}1,\Gamma_L)\) and \(\mathcal{Q}\) with \((D_Q/D_L,\gamma)\), the basal source entering as a \(\delta(z)\) surface flux.

Cells are flagged emitting by the relay gate alone (\(\mathcal{L}>1\)), not by the usual "source weight \(>\tfrac12\)" convention: above \(\tilde\sigma^\star\) the tone exceeds 1 everywhere, which would flag every cell silent and make the measured front — and every swept \(\tilde c\) — collapse to zero for a purely bookkeeping reason. Emission strength is shown as dot/halo opacity instead.

Left: the LTB4 relay \(\mathcal{L}\) (magma). Right: the quorum field \(\mathcal{Q}\) (greens), each panel normalised to its own maximum in that frame — the \(\mathcal{Q}\) panel is about structure (where the signal piles up), not absolute level; read the level off the \(\mathcal{Q}(\tilde r)\) profile below. Cells are drawn identically in both: bright = over the relay threshold and firing, dimmed in proportion to its own throttle factor \(H^-(\mathcal{Q};1;m)\).

Channelisation \(\Psi(\tilde t)\) — does the swarm break into radial channels? Streaming shows up as structure in the angular distribution of cells, so it is measured from the azimuthal Fourier modes of the cell angles (cells outside a small excluded core, \(\tilde r > 0.1\tilde R_{\text{dish}}\)):

\[ c_m = \frac1N\sum_i e^{im\theta_i}, \qquad \Psi_m = \sqrt{\max\!\left(0,\ \frac{N|c_m|^2-1}{N-1}\right)} \ \in[0,1]. \]

The \(-1\) subtracts the shot-noise floor \(E[|c_m|^2]=1/N\) that even a perfectly uniform swarm produces, which is what makes \(\Psi\) comparable across densities: \(\Psi_m=0\) is angularly uniform, \(\Psi_m=1\) is every cell at one angle with \(m\)-fold periodicity. Left: \(\Psi=\max_{m\ge2}\Psi_m\) versus time — one number for "how channelised is it". Right: the whole spectrum \(\Psi_m\) at the scrubbed time; the dashed line is the 5% shot-noise level \(\sqrt{2/(N-1)}\) (bars below it mean nothing) and the highlighted dominant mode \(m^\star\) is the channel count — six spokes peak at \(m=6\). \(m=1\) is greyed out and excluded from \(\Psi\): it measures a bulk off-centre drift of the whole swarm, not spokes.

This is a cell-position diagnostic, identical on every Setup-4 page, so the comparison that matters is across pages at the same \(\tilde\sigma\): M1 is the channelising baseline the M6 variants are meant to suppress.

Density sweep \(\tilde c(\tilde\sigma)\). A multi-run diagnostic (its own Sweep c(σ̃) button, separate from Calculate): for each density it runs a short simulation and measures the relay front speed. The panel shows the measured points only — no analytical \(c(\tilde\sigma)\) curve is drawn, because the mean-field pushed-front flux balance does not describe this system reliably and plotting it only invited reading the simulation as a deviation from a law that was never right. Compare sweeps instead: one at \(\beta=0\) (bare relay), one at \(m=1\), one at \(m=2\). The vertical marker shows where the single run on this page sits along the swept axis.

Point colours encode why a point is what it is. Filled orange: a timed relay front. Filled red: no self-sustaining wave — the fired blob merely spreads. Hollow amber: a real but very slow front that did not clear the ignition halo within \(\tilde t_{\text{max}}\) (a window limit, not a zero) — expect these wherever the front becomes slow, e.g. at large \(\beta\) or large \(m\). Hollow gray: the ignition stimulus alone would flood the dish, so nothing can be timed.

The sweep keeps the injected \(\mathcal{L}\) mass fixed across densities (\(s_{\text{fire}}\propto1/\tilde\sigma\)) so the ignition halo has the same radius at every point, and times the front only outside that halo. Without this, a strong stimulus at high density spreads diffusively across the whole dish and is mistimed as a wave — a fully throttled control run (relay impossible) then reports a finite speed. The measured points still carry the usual discrete-cell offset below the mean-field curve.
