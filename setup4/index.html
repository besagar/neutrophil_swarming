Setup 4a — Emergent-wave swarming · M1 (basic relay)

A disk of \(N\) neutrophils sits in a petri dish of nondim radius \(\tilde{R}_{\text{dish}}\). Each cell emits LTB4 at rate proportional to \(H^+(\mathcal{L};\,1;\,n_L)\) when the local nondim concentration \(\mathcal{L} = L/L_0\) exceeds the relay threshold \(L_0\). The cue field \(\mathcal{L}\) satisfies a diffusion PDE, so the wave is emergent — its amplitude and speed are outputs, not inputs.

Model M1 (basic relay). The nondim cue PDE (discrete-ABM form) is:

\[ \partial_{\tilde{t}}\mathcal{L} = \tilde{\nabla}^2\mathcal{L} + \frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\sum_i H^+(\mathcal{L}_i;\,1;\,n_L)\, \tilde{\delta}^2(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_i) - \Gamma_L\,\mathcal{L} \qquad \text{(2D–2D)} \] \[ \partial_{\tilde{t}}\mathcal{L} = \tilde{\nabla}^2\mathcal{L} + \tilde{\delta}(\tilde{z})\sum_i H^+(\mathcal{L}_i;\,1;\,n_L)\, \tilde{\delta}^2(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_i) - \Gamma_L\,\mathcal{L} \qquad \text{(2D–3D)} \]

where \(H^+(x;\,x_0;\,n) = x^n/(x_0^n+x^n)\) is the smoothed Heaviside gate, \(\Gamma_L \geq 0\) is optional LTB4 decay, and the per-cell prefactor is \(1\) (2D–3D) or \(1/\tilde{h}\) (2D–2D). The source is smeared onto the grid via bilinear particle-in-cell (PIC) interpolation. Absorbing BC: \(\mathcal{L}=0\) at the dish edge \(\tilde{r}=\tilde{R}_{\text{dish}}\).

Length and time scales. The nondimensionalization uses intrinsic (cell-level) scales:

\[ \ell_0 = \frac{a}{L_0\,D_L}, \qquad t_0 = \frac{\ell_0^2}{D_L} = \frac{a^2}{L_0^2\,D_L^3}. \]

\(\ell_0\) is the distance at which a single emitter saturates a relay-threshold profile in 3D (up to a \(4\pi\) factor) — the single-cell influence radius. \(\sigma\) does not appear in the unit system; instead, cell density enters as the nondim group \(\tilde{\sigma} = \sigma\,\ell_0^2\). The measured wave speed in nondim units is \(\tilde{c}_{\text{Dieterle}} = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\,\tilde{\sigma}\) (2D–3D continuum limit) or \(\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}/\tilde{h}}\times\tfrac{2}{\pi}\) (2D–2D). When \(\tilde{\sigma} \ll 1\) the simulation is in the discrete-cell regime (jagged single-cell hotspots); \(\tilde{\sigma} \gtrsim 1\) is the continuum regime where the Dieterle analytical overlay matches.

GL cell SDE (Euler–Maruyama). Polarization \(\mathbf{P}_i\) and position \(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i\):

\[ d\mathbf{P}_i = \Bigl[ \kappa\,\tilde{\nabla}\mathcal{L} + \lambda\,(\mathcal{L} - \mathcal{L}_c)\,\mathbf{P}_i + \nu\,(|\mathbf{P}_i|^2 - |\mathbf{P}_i|^4)\,\mathbf{P}_i \Bigr]d\tilde{t} + \sqrt{2\,\vartheta\,\mathcal{L}\,d\tilde{t}}\;\boldsymbol{\eta}_i, \] \[ \frac{d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i}{d\tilde{t}} = \mu\,\mathbf{P}_i. \]

The four drift coefficients \(\lambda,\nu,\kappa,\mu\) and the noise coefficient \(\vartheta\) are all independent dim-derived nondim groups (no \(\Lambda\) wrapper).

\(\lambda\)linear activation strength \(\nu\)GL nonlinearity strength \(\kappa\)chemotactic coupling \(\mu\)cell motility \(\vartheta\)noise strength \(\mathcal{L}_c\)cell threshold / relay threshold \(\tilde{\sigma}\)nondim cell density \((= \sigma\,\ell_0^2)\) \(\tilde{h}\)nondim layer height (2D–2D only) \(n_L\)Hill exponent (relay gate; \(n\to\infty\) → step) \(\Gamma_L\)LTB4 decay rate \(\tilde{R}_{\text{target}}\)target radius (nondim, in \(\ell_0\)); cells that reach it stick to it

Target — “stick to the target”. A circle of radius \(\tilde{R}_{\text{target}}\) at the dish centre stands for the object the swarm converges on (a candida cluster, a bead, a sterile injury). It is a one-way, absorbing inner boundary: the first time a cell reaches \(\tilde r_i \le \tilde{R}_{\text{target}}\) it is projected onto the circle and frozen there for the rest of the run — it has engaged the target. A stuck cell still polarizes and still emits the cue, so the cue model above is untouched; only \(d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i/d\tilde{t} = \mu\mathbf{P}_i\) is suppressed. The target disk is excluded from the initial cell placement, so no cell starts engaged. Switch it off in the controls to recover the plain dish (reflective outer wall only). Engaged cells are ringed in teal on the dish, and the on target KPI is the recruitment curve. See setup4_swarm3d.md §2c.

Interaction paradigm. The PDE loop is too expensive for real-time update. Set all parameters, press Calculate, and the simulation runs in a Web Worker saving frames. When done, use the time slider to scrub through the trajectory.

Numerical methods

2D–2D: explicit Euler for diffusion, sub-stepped at the CFL limit \(d\tilde{t}_{\text{sub}} \leq \Delta\tilde{x}^2/4\). Number of substeps: \(n_{\text{sub}} = \lceil d\tilde{t} / (\Delta\tilde{x}^2/4) \rceil\).

2D–3D: operator split — (i) explicit Euler for \(x,y\) diffusion (sub-stepped on \(\Delta\tilde{x}^2/4\) CFL); (ii) Crank–Nicolson for \(z\) diffusion (tridiagonal Thomas algorithm, unconditionally stable, avoids the prohibitively small \(d\tilde{t} \leq h_0^2/6\) explicit CFL from the fine near-wall spacing). Non-uniform \(z\)-grid: \(\Delta z_j = h_0\,\alpha^j\). Neumann BC at \(z=0\) and \(z=z_{\max}\). The \(\delta(\tilde{z})\) source is discretized as a volumetric term at \(z=0\) nodes with coefficient \(2/h_0\) (half-space control-volume normalization, see setup4_cue_models.md §1b).

Cell–field coupling uses bilinear PIC: identical weights for both interpolating \(\mathcal{L}\) at the agent position and smearing the emission back onto grid nodes — consistent adjoint pair (no flux bias).

Channelisation \(\Psi(\tilde t)\) — does the swarm break into radial channels? Streaming shows up as structure in the angular distribution of cells, so it is measured from the azimuthal Fourier modes of the cell angles (cells outside a small excluded core, \(\tilde r > 0.1\tilde R_{\text{dish}}\)):

\[ c_m = \frac1N\sum_i e^{im\theta_i}, \qquad \Psi_m = \sqrt{\max\!\left(0,\ \frac{N|c_m|^2-1}{N-1}\right)} \ \in[0,1]. \]

The \(-1\) subtracts the shot-noise floor \(E[|c_m|^2]=1/N\) that even a perfectly uniform swarm produces, which is what makes \(\Psi\) comparable across densities: \(\Psi_m=0\) is angularly uniform, \(\Psi_m=1\) is every cell at one angle with \(m\)-fold periodicity. Left: \(\Psi=\max_{m\ge2}\Psi_m\) versus time — one number for "how channelised is it". Right: the whole spectrum \(\Psi_m\) at the scrubbed time; the dashed line is the 5% shot-noise level \(\sqrt{2/(N-1)}\) (bars below it mean nothing) and the highlighted dominant mode \(m^\star\) is the channel count — six spokes peak at \(m=6\). \(m=1\) is greyed out and excluded from \(\Psi\): it measures a bulk off-centre drift of the whole swarm, not spokes.

This is a cell-position diagnostic, identical on every Setup-4 page, so the comparison that matters is across pages at the same \(\tilde\sigma\): M1 is the channelising baseline the M6 variants are meant to suppress.

\[ F(\mathbf{P}) = -\!\left[\lambda\,(\mathcal{L} - \mathcal{L}_c)\,\frac{|\mathbf{P}|^2}{2} + \nu\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{P}|^4}{4} - \frac{|\mathbf{P}|^6}{6}\right)\right] \;-\; \kappa\;\nabla\mathcal{L}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{P}. \]

The last term tilts the otherwise rotationally-symmetric Mexican-hat well, lowering one side and raising the other. The yellow arrow in each panel points along \(\nabla\mathcal{L}\): cells preferentially polarize against it (anti-wave regime). Left: 3D wireframe of \(F(P_x, P_y)\) with the bead. Middle: 2D phase plane with contour lines (cool colours above the well, warm below) — note how they become non-circular when \(\nabla\mathcal{L} \neq 0\). The dashed white ring is the no-tilt equilibrium \(|P|_{\text{eq}}\), shown for reference. Bottom: 1-D cross-section \(F(s)\) along the \(\nabla\mathcal{L}\) axis — the asymmetric double-well that drives chemotaxis. \(s\) is the projection of \(\mathbf{P}\) onto \(\hat{\nabla}\mathcal{L}\); the red bead is the cell's current position on this slice.
