Setup 4 — Emergent-wave swarming (Model M1)

A disk of \(N\) neutrophils sits in a petri dish of nondim radius \(\tilde{R}_{\text{dish}}\). Each cell emits LTB4 at rate proportional to \(H^+(\mathcal{L};\,1;\,n_L)\) when the local nondim concentration \(\mathcal{L} = L/L_0\) exceeds the relay threshold \(L_0\). The cue field \(\mathcal{L}\) satisfies a diffusion PDE, so the wave is emergent — its amplitude and speed are outputs, not inputs.

Model M1 (basic relay). The nondim cue equations are:

\[ \partial_{\tilde{t}}\mathcal{L} = \tilde{\nabla}^2_{2D}\mathcal{L} + H^+(\mathcal{L};\,1;\,n_L) - \tilde{\Gamma}_L\,\mathcal{L} \qquad \text{(2D–2D)} \] \[ \partial_{\tilde{t}}\mathcal{L} = \tilde{\nabla}^2_{3D}\mathcal{L} + H^+(\mathcal{L};\,1;\,n_L)\,\tilde{\delta}(\tilde{z}) - \tilde{\Gamma}_L\,\mathcal{L} \qquad \text{(2D–3D)} \]

where \(H^+(x;\,x_0;\,n) = x^n/(x_0^n+x^n)\) is the smoothed Heaviside gate and \(\tilde{\Gamma}_L \geq 0\) is optional LTB4 decay. The cell emission source is smeared onto the grid via bilinear particle-in-cell (PIC) interpolation. Absorbing BC: \(\mathcal{L}=0\) at the dish edge \(\tilde{r}=\tilde{R}_{\text{dish}}\).

Length and time scales. The nondimensionalization uses the M1 parametric wave speed \(c^*\):

\[ c^* = \sqrt{\frac{a\,\sigma\,D_L}{h\,L_0}}\;(\text{2D–2D}), \qquad c^* = \frac{a\,\sigma}{L_0}\;(\text{2D–3D}), \qquad \ell_0 = \frac{D_L}{c^*},\quad t_0 = \frac{D_L}{c^{*2}}. \]

With this choice \(c^* = 1\) in nondim units. The measured wave speed approaches \(1\) (2D–2D) or \(2/\pi \approx 0.637\) (2D–3D) in the continuum limit.

GL cell SDE (Euler–Maruyama). Polarization \(\mathbf{P}_i\) and position \(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i\):

\[ d\mathbf{P}_i = \Bigl[ \tilde{\chi}\,\tilde{\nabla}\mathcal{L} + \Lambda\bigl((\mathcal{L} - \tilde{\mathcal{L}}_c)\,\mathbf{P}_i + \lambda(|\mathbf{P}_i|^2 - |\mathbf{P}_i|^4)\,\mathbf{P}_i\bigr) \Bigr]d\tilde{t} + \sqrt{2\,\Lambda\,\vartheta\,\mathcal{L}\,d\tilde{t}}\;\boldsymbol{\eta}_i, \] \[ \frac{d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_i}{d\tilde{t}} = \tilde{\mu}\,\mathbf{P}_i. \]

\(\Lambda = r_0 L_c D_L/c^{*2}\) controls how fast cells respond relative to the wave timescale. \(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_c = L_c/L_0\) is the cell polarization threshold relative to the relay threshold.

\(\Lambda\)wave time / cell activation time \(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_c\)cell threshold / relay threshold \(\tilde{\chi}\)chemotactic coupling \(\tilde{\mu}\)cell motility \(\lambda\)GL well depth (intrinsic) \(\vartheta\)noise strength (intrinsic) \(n_L\)Hill exponent (relay gate; \(n\to\infty\) → step) \(\tilde{\Gamma}_L\)LTB4 decay rate

Interaction paradigm. The PDE loop is too expensive for real-time update. Set all parameters, press Calculate, and the simulation runs in a Web Worker saving frames. When done, use the time slider to scrub through the trajectory.

Numerical methods

2D–2D: explicit Euler for diffusion, sub-stepped at the CFL limit \(d\tilde{t}_{\text{sub}} \leq \Delta\tilde{x}^2/4\). Number of substeps: \(n_{\text{sub}} = \lceil d\tilde{t} / (\Delta\tilde{x}^2/4) \rceil\).

2D–3D: operator split — (i) explicit Euler for \(x,y\) diffusion (sub-stepped on \(\Delta\tilde{x}^2/4\) CFL); (ii) Crank–Nicolson for \(z\) diffusion (tridiagonal Thomas algorithm, unconditionally stable, avoids the prohibitively small \(d\tilde{t} \leq h_0^2/6\) explicit CFL from the fine near-wall spacing). Non-uniform \(z\)-grid: \(\Delta z_j = h_0\,\alpha^j\). Neumann BC at \(z=0\) and \(z=z_{\max}\). The \(\delta(\tilde{z})\) source is discretized as a volumetric term at \(z=0\) nodes with coefficient \(2/h_0\) (half-space control-volume normalization, see setup4_cue_models.md §1b).

Cell–field coupling uses bilinear PIC: identical weights for both interpolating \(\mathcal{L}\) at the agent position and smearing the emission back onto grid nodes — consistent adjoint pair (no flux bias).

Bead-in-free-energy view. Pick a cell with the tracked cell slider — it appears as a large cyan crosshair on the dish above. The three panels below show its polarization \(P\) in the Ginzburg-Landau free energy *with* the chemotactic tilt:

\[ F(\mathbf{P}) = -\Lambda\!\left[(\mathcal{L} - \tilde{\mathcal{L}}_c)\,|\mathbf{P}|^2/2 + \lambda\!\left(|\mathbf{P}|^4/4 - |\mathbf{P}|^6/6\right)\right] \;-\; \tilde{\chi}\;\nabla\mathcal{L}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{P}. \]

The last term tilts the otherwise rotationally-symmetric Mexican-hat well, lowering one side and raising the other. The yellow arrow in each panel points along \(\nabla\mathcal{L}\): cells preferentially polarize against it (anti-wave regime). Left: 3D wireframe of \(F(P_x, P_y)\) with the bead. The dashed white ring is the no-tilt equilibrium \(|P|_{\text{eq}}\), shown for reference. Bottom: 1-D cross-section \(F(s)\) along the \(\nabla\mathcal{L}\) axis — the asymmetric double-well that drives chemotaxis. \(s\) is the projection of \(\mathbf{P}\) onto \(\hat{\nabla}\mathcal{L}\); the red bead is the cell's current position on this slice.
